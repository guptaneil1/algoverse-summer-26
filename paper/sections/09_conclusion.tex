\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper defines an evidence-gated investigation of finite human-data allocation in
recursive training: a fixed lifetime budget of human-origin optimizer tokens, four
budget-matched treatment families, and complete recursive chains as experimental units. The
allocation rule, hyperparameters, seed set, outcomes, and interpretation language were
frozen before any primary execution, using interfaces, fixtures, and synthetic results.

The pilot ran to completion, and the preregistered fairness constraint rejected it. Equal
lifetime human-origin spend failed on one arm; equal total optimizer tokens failed across
arms, on an axis the launcher had never asserted. \textbf{We report the primary contrast as
not established.} We did not repair it, reweight it, or promote the secondary comparison
that would have supported the paper's hypothesis --- and that comparison was available,
sizeable, and tempting. Declining it is the substantive claim this work can make about its
own conduct.

What the work does establish is narrower and worth stating plainly. The pipeline reproduces
a published recursive-training result on real assets, across two independent executions on
different hardware and dependency stacks. The apparatus runs the full recursive allocation
loop end to end and emits certifiable artifacts. One budget-matched comparison survives: at
this budget and horizon, when a fixed human budget is spent does not detectably change the
outcome, an interval containing zero and lying inside the practical equivalence region.
Between-chain variance is small enough --- coefficients of variation from \PilotCVLow\% to
\PilotCVHigh\% against a \PilotThresholdPct\% threshold --- that the frozen seed set is
already powered at the preregistered effect size. That inverts the assumption the pilot was
commissioned to test: for this design, chain count is not the binding constraint on a
powered experiment, and the constraint lies elsewhere.

It lies in the design. Measurement showed that adding human data to a fixed synthetic
corpus makes total training volume depend on how much a policy spends, so allocation
strategy and data quantity cannot be separated --- including in the comparison against a
control that spends nothing. We therefore specify that rescued human examples \emph{displace}
synthetic records rather than being appended to them, which makes the total-token condition
hold by construction rather than by measurement. That specification is untested; no chain
has yet run under it.

The remaining obstacle is neither compute nor a design freeze. The estimand, contrast, seed
set, and interpretation templates are fixed; the defects the pilot exposed are closed and
pinned by regression tests; and a powered run is affordable at the measured cost. What
remains is to validate the displacement specification on a single chain and then execute
the grid with both fairness axes asserted. Until that happens, the hypotheses that motivated
this work stay untested, and this paper claims nothing about them.
