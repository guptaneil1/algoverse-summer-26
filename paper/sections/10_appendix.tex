\section{Experimental Apparatus}
\label{app:apparatus}

Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} in Section~\ref{sec:intro} shows the recursive procedure. One
\emph{chain} is one independently seeded traversal of the loop for the full horizon $G$.
Chains, not generations, are the experimental units: generations within a chain are repeated
observations of the same trajectory and are not independent samples.

\section{Positive Control}
\label{app:positive-control}

Before any treatment comparison, the pipeline is validated against a published result:
Drayson et al.'s recursive-training study, at upstream commit
\texttt{feb8511479a2e2dc868e1caf3f63cb99f1fcc746}, GPT-2 on WikiText-2 over eleven
generations, with the paper's importance-sampling mitigation disabled so that the
unmitigated baseline is what is measured.

Both arms complete every planned generation. Per-generation perplexity and the resulting
degradation ratios are given in Table~\ref{tab:positive-control}, generated from the
committed run artifacts. The fully synthetic arm ($\alpha=0$) degrades substantially; the
human-mixed arm ($\alpha=1$) is close to flat. The synthetic arm lies above the mixed arm at
every subsequent generation and the two never re-cross, so the expected ordering and
degradation direction are recovered.

Two independent executions were performed, on different accelerators and different
dependency stacks. Every reported quantity agrees between them to well under one per cent,
including the case where the two arms computed their shared baseline generation separately.
The per-execution values and their differences are recorded in the evidence package rather
than restated here.

Agreement with the published values is assessed against a five per cent relative difference.
This is an internal engineering tolerance, adopted because the source provides no suitable
uncertainty for the endpoint, and it must not be read as proof of exact replication. All
deviations from the upstream configuration are enumerated in the accompanying evidence
record.

Stage A uses WikiText-2, the upstream default. The pilot design targets a larger corpus
from the same family; the two stages therefore use different corpus sizes, recorded as a
deviation before execution rather than after.

The published positive control reproduces qualitatively. Both arms complete every planned
generation; the expected arm ordering and degradation direction are recovered; a clean
rerun on independent hardware reaches the same conclusion, with every reported quantity
agreeing to well under one per cent across the two executions; all deviations are
enumerated; and the reported table is regenerated from saved metrics by the same command
that produced them, with every recorded artifact hash verified.

Agreement with the published endpoint is assessed against a five per cent relative
difference adopted as an internal engineering tolerance. It is not proof of exact
replication, and no downstream claim rests on it.

This establishes that the pipeline reproduces a known result on real assets. Together with
the executed grid it establishes that the apparatus runs the full recursive allocation loop
end to end on real models under matched budgets and produces certifiable artifacts.

\section{Executed Configuration}
\label{app:config}

The pilot configuration was frozen before any chain ran, and every scientific value in it
traces to written evidence recorded at freeze time: the retraining regime, the corpus
subsample, the lifetime and per-generation budgets, the ordered seed set, the horizon, the
outcome definitions, the contrast, and the exclusion rules. Two further reference
configurations remain unfrozen and the runner continues to refuse them.

The executed grid is the five treatment families of Section~\ref{sec:method} crossed with
the five preregistered seeds: twenty-five chains at horizon ten, GPT-2 at a pinned
revision, WikiText-103 subsampled to four hundred articles from the frozen base-training
partition, a lifetime budget of $750{,}000$ human-origin optimizer tokens per chain, and
generation prompts drawn from a partition held disjoint from training data. Execution took
\PilotWallHours{} hours across four accelerators.

Budget matching is asserted after execution rather than assumed, over the whole grid rather
than per shard, and a violation is a non-zero exit rather than a warning. That assertion is
what determines the status of the primary comparison in Section~\ref{sec:results}, and it
did not pass.

\section{Data Separation}
\label{app:separation}

Each example receives a stable content hash before splitting. Five partitions are held
disjoint: base human training data, per-generation rescue candidates, generation prompts,
validation data, and final human test data. No final test example may influence prompting,
selection, threshold tuning, early stopping, or hyperparameter choice.

\section{Data Appendix}
\label{app:data}

The domain is frozen and was executed. The corpus is WikiText-103, subsampled to
the first 400 \texttt{base\_train} articles, tokenized with the GPT-2 tokenizer at
revision \texttt{607a30d7} into blocks of 512 tokens. Each generation's decode
emits roughly 3{,}280 records, and the assembled training corpus holds exactly that
many in every generation after the first, because rescued human examples displace
synthetic records one for one rather than being appended.

The released artifacts carry the following, and this appendix indexes them rather
than restating values that would then exist in two places:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Dataset identifier, revision hash, and licence, with the licence audit
    that selected it over the audited alternatives.
  \item Preprocessing: tokenizer identity and revision, normalisation, and the
    exact filter chain, each with a content hash.
  \item Partition statistics for all five partitions --- base training, rescue
    candidates, prompts, validation, and final test --- reporting example counts,
    token counts, and per-partition manifest hashes.
  \item The frozen mode definition, the number of modes $M$, and the criterion
    separating tail modes from common modes.
  \item Overlap report: exact and near-duplicate checks across every partition
    pair, with the near-duplicate threshold declared before it was applied.
\end{itemize}

Final test data influences no prompt, selection decision, threshold, stopping
rule, or hyperparameter. This constraint is enforced by the disjointness tests
rather than asserted.

\section{Evaluation Appendix}
\label{app:evaluation}

The tail-retention definition is frozen: the ratio-based metric, selected over
\texttt{nll\_gap} on reliability and independence grounds and recorded as D-022.
Both candidates were implemented and audited for determinism, independence from the
policy's selection signal, sensitivity to tail degradation, discriminant validity
against non-tail modes, and split-half reliability; the frozen choice is the one
reported throughout this paper. Mode scores are a monotone-decreasing transform of
mean held-out negative log-likelihood rather than raw likelihood, and the
generation-0 value is snapshotted as the reference every later generation is
measured against.

Held-out human negative log-likelihood is computed from target-token log
probabilities on the frozen test partition. Regret is aggregated across the
horizon as the area under the generation-wise curve.

Both tail candidates are computed without reference to the policy's
under-coverage score, so no policy can be rewarded for agreeing with its own
selection signal. This is a structural property of the interfaces, not a
convention.

\section{Statistics Appendix}
\label{app:statistics}

Uncertainty is computed across independent chains. Multiple generations within one
chain are repeated observations and do not increase the effective sample size.

The primary contrast is the joint policy against the strongest eligible non-joint
baseline, paired by chain seed. Paired differences are summarised by their mean
with a two-sided $95\%$ Student $t$ interval on four degrees of freedom, the frozen
seed set being five chains per arm. The smallest scientifically meaningful effect
was frozen before outcomes were opened (U-006) at $2\%$ relative, taken against the
fresh-random arm's mean regret, and the interpretation that follows is selected
mechanically from precommitted templates covering favourable, null, harmful, and
uncertain outcomes.

Which arm satisfies ``strongest eligible non-joint baseline'' is read from the
outcome rather than fixed in advance; the selection rule is what was preregistered.
This is the conservative direction, since it compares the joint policy against the
best baseline available to it rather than a convenient one.

\section{Reproducibility Appendix}
\label{app:reproducibility}

Every value reported in this paper is generated by a script and traced to an
immutable artifact. No number is entered by hand.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Positive control.} Upstream repository and exact commit are
    pinned before execution, together with the resolved framework versions and
    tokenizer revision actually used. Deviations from the upstream configuration
    are declared before running, never after observing a result.
  \item \textbf{Run artifacts.} Each chain emits a run manifest and a chain
    result validated against versioned JSON schemas. A three-state validator
    classifies each run as valid, valid with limitation, or invalid; blocking
    failures include budget-equality violations, identity mismatches, and
    impossible token accounting.
  \item \textbf{Budget matching.} Total optimizer tokens are identical across every
    policy in a comparison, by construction: rescued human examples displace
    synthetic records rather than being appended, so each generation's training
    volume is fixed regardless of what a policy buys. Lifetime human-origin tokens
    are matched as a common ceiling reached up to indivisibility rather than as
    exact equality, since candidates are indivisible and a policy cannot spend a
    fraction of one; the realised spread is reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}
    and is an order of magnitude below the practical effect threshold. Both axes
    are checked before launch from configuration and again after the run from
    emitted artifacts.
  \item \textbf{Token accounting.} Counts are denominated in tokens consumed by the
    optimizer rather than in documents. Repeated presentation of an example costs
    its tokens once per presentation; gradient accumulation counts every
    micro-batch; resume neither double-counts nor drops the batch spanning a
    checkpoint. These properties are asserted by the accounting tests on fixtures.
    On the executed run they are \emph{not} recomputed from realised batch records,
    which were not retained: the declared ledger is checked against the frozen
    budget instead. Every chain carries this as a recorded limitation rather than
    as a silent assumption.
  \item \textbf{Determinism.} Seeds propagate through sampling, generation,
    initialisation, dropout, and evaluation. Checkpoints carry content digests so
    silent corruption cannot survive a resume.
  \item \textbf{Generated outputs.} Tables and figures are emitted by scripts and
    recorded with SHA-256 digests in an artifact manifest, so a reader can verify
    that a figure is the one the pipeline produced.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations of Scope}
\label{app:scope}

The executed study covers one licensed domain, one 124M-parameter screening model,
and one horizon of ten generations. It cannot establish behaviour at larger scales,
in other domains, or for other model families. Any claim beyond the tested
configuration is future work and is marked as such.

Three of the seven comparators the claim contract names were not implemented:
accumulation or fixed-fraction mixing, detector-based or importance-resampling
selection, and oracle mode information as a non-deployable upper bound. The absent
oracle bounds every result reported here, because an equivalence between two
policies carries no information about how much of the achievable gain either
captures.
