\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{What was executed}

The pilot grid completed: five policies crossed with the \PilotSeeds{} preregistered
seeds, \PilotChains{} independently seeded chains, each run to the frozen horizon of
\PilotHorizon{} generations, in \PilotWallHours{} hours of wall time on four
accelerators. Every chain completed and none failed.

Certification is mixed, and the split is informative. Fifteen chains classify
\texttt{valid\_with\_limitation}; ten classify \texttt{invalid} on budget grounds --- the
five joint chains on both budget axes, and the five selection-only chains on total
optimizer tokens alone. The limitations recorded on the remaining fifteen are that the
realised token ledger was compared against the frozen budget rather than recomputed from
batch records, and that near-duplicate coverage was not rescanned at validation time;
neither is blocking. The budget failures are, and the subsections below treat them as
such.

\subsection{The primary comparison is not established}

The preregistered primary contrast is the paired chain-level difference between the joint
policy and the strongest eligible non-joint baseline. \textbf{This run does not support
it}, and the reason is a violation of the fairness constraint rather than an absence of
signal.

Realised lifetime human-origin spend was not matched across arms. Four of the five
policies consumed between \PilotMatchedLow{} and \PilotMatchedHigh{} optimizer tokens
against a \PilotCeiling{} ceiling, a spread of \PilotMatchedSpreadPct\% and within one
indivisible candidate. The joint policy consumed \PilotJointSpend{} at every seed --- a
shortfall of \PilotJointShortfall{} tokens, or \PilotJointShortfallPct\%, where the
largest single candidate in the frozen pool costs a small fraction of that.
Indivisibility does not explain a gap of this size.

The cause is an implementation defect in terminal budget reconciliation, found after the
run and recorded in the project's failure log. The reconciliation raised the joint
policy's per-generation spending cap at the final spending generation but left its
feasibility floor near zero, so its urgency rule rather than the reconciliation determined
the spend; at low monitored urgency that rule declines to spend at all. The other three
spending policies are governed by their allowance alone and were unaffected.

A comparison in which one arm received a tenth less human data cannot separate the effect
of allocation strategy from the effect of data quantity. Following the preregistered
disposition for a violated fairness constraint, the contrast is reported as invalid rather
than repaired, reweighted, or reported with a caveat. \textbf{We make no claim about the
joint policy relative to any baseline.} The defect is fixed and pinned by regression test;
establishing the contrast requires re-running the grid, which was outside the compute
budget available for this work.

\subsection{Between-chain variance}

The pilot's stated purpose was to estimate between-chain variance so that a powered study
can be sized; the project's compute gate blocks that study until the estimate exists. This
quantity is measured \emph{within} arms and is therefore unaffected by the cross-arm
budget gaps.

Between-chain variation is substantially smaller than the design assumed. Coefficients of
variation on the primary outcome range from \PilotCVLow\% to \PilotCVHigh\% across the
five arms, against a preregistered practical effect threshold of \PilotThresholdPct\%. The
paired difference between the joint policy and the best-performing baseline has a standard
deviation of \PilotPairedSD{} in outcome units, where the practical threshold corresponds
to \PilotThresholdUnits{} in those units --- several times larger than the noise it must
be distinguished from.

The consequence is that the frozen seed set already provides roughly $80\%$ power to
detect an effect at the preregistered threshold. Chain count is therefore not the binding
constraint on a powered experiment, which inverts the planning assumption this pilot was
commissioned to test. We report this as the pilot's substantive deliverable.

One caveat bounds it: the joint policy's own variance was measured on chains that
underspent, so whether a correctly spending joint policy exhibits comparable variance is
not established here. The four other arms spent as designed and their variance estimates
carry no such qualification.

\subsection{Secondary comparisons, and a second constraint}

The fairness constraint has two axes: matched lifetime human-origin tokens \emph{and}
matched total optimizer tokens. Only the first was asserted before or during the run.
Excluding the joint policy, the remaining chains satisfy that first axis at a
\PilotMatchedSpreadPct\% spread --- but realised \emph{totals} span \PilotTotalSpreadPct\% across arms,
above the practical threshold. Applying both axes pairwise leaves exactly one matched
contrast.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Schedule-only against fresh random} is matched on both axes (human
      \PilotSchedRandHumanPct\%, total \PilotSchedRandTotalPct\%). It does \emph{not} differ
      detectably:
      \PilotSchedRandPct\%, $95\%$ CI $[\PilotSchedRandLow, \PilotSchedRandHigh]$, an
      interval containing zero and lying inside the practical equivalence region.
      \textbf{At this budget and horizon, when the budget is spent does not detectably
      change the outcome.} This is the one comparison the run supports, and it is a null.

\item \textbf{Selection-only against fresh random} shows a \PilotSelRandPct\% advantage,
      $95\%$ CI $[\PilotSelRandLow, \PilotSelRandHigh]$, but arrives with
      \PilotSelRandTotalPct\% more
      total training tokens in the same direction as the effect. At roughly five times the
      confound it is suggestive; it is not budget-matched, and we do not report it as a
      result. Separating selection from training volume requires a run in which totals are
      matched by construction.

\item \textbf{Spending against not spending} improves the outcome by
      \PilotRandNonePct\%, $95\%$ CI $[\PilotRandNoneLow, \PilotRandNoneHigh]$, measured
      against a control that by construction spends no human tokens and is therefore not
      budget-matched to anything.
\end{itemize}

That the total-token axis went unasserted is a finding about the apparatus rather than
about allocation, and we report it as one: a guard that checks one of two named conditions
reports success for the one it checks. It is recorded in the project's failure log, and
the check now asserts both axes.

\paragraph{These are secondary and cannot substitute for the primary analysis.} The
preregistration states that there is one central budget, horizon, primary outcome and
primary contrast, and that other comparisons are secondary or exploratory and cannot
replace a failed primary analysis. The primary contrast requires the joint policy and is
not established. Promoting any comparison above into a confirmatory finding would be
exactly the substitution the preregistration was written to prevent, and we do not make
it.

Four further limits apply. The comparisons hold at one budget, one horizon, one corpus and
one model. The seeds were frozen for a different contrast. Selection-only is measured
against random selection rather than against a strong alternative selection rule. And no
oracle upper bound was implemented, so the headroom remaining above any of these policies
is unknown.

\subsection{Positive control}

The published positive control reproduces qualitatively. Both arms complete every planned
generation; the expected arm ordering and degradation direction are recovered; a clean
rerun on independent hardware reaches the same conclusion, with every reported quantity
agreeing to well under one per cent across the two executions; all deviations are
enumerated; and the reported table is regenerated from saved metrics by the same command
that produced them, with every recorded artifact hash verified.

Agreement with the published endpoint is assessed against a five per cent relative
difference adopted as an internal engineering tolerance. It is not proof of exact
replication, and no downstream claim rests on it.

This establishes that the pipeline reproduces a known result on real assets. Together with
the executed pilot it also establishes that the apparatus runs the full recursive
allocation loop end to end on real models and produces certifiable artifacts. Neither
establishes anything about allocation policy, which is the question this paper poses and
does not answer.
