\title{Bounding a Self-Consuming Failure Mode on a Budget:\\
Where Scarce Human Data Should Go in Recursive Training}

\author{Anonymous Submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A model trained on its own outputs is a self-evolving system with a known failure mode:
across generations the learned distribution drifts and its low-probability regions thin
out. Human-origin data bounds that failure, but it is finite. We ask where a \emph{fixed
lifetime budget} of human-origin optimizer tokens should go --- \emph{when} in the chain,
and \emph{which} degraded regions --- and answer it with \PilotChains{} independently
seeded chains matched on both lifetime human tokens and total optimizer tokens. The
preregistered contrast is a \textbf{null}: adapting both axes jointly does not improve on
adapting only the distributional one (\PilotPrimaryMean{}, $95\%$~CI
$[\PilotPrimaryLow, \PilotPrimaryHigh]$), with the interval lying entirely inside the
frozen equivalence region rather than merely spanning zero. The secondary contrasts locate
the effect: spending at all is worth \PilotRandNonePct\% against a control trained on
\emph{identical} data volume, targeting degraded modes a further \PilotSelRandPct\%, and
\emph{when} to spend \PilotSchedRandPct\% with an interval containing zero. Diversity-aware
targeting is what bounds the failure; scheduling is not, on the primary outcome. Three of
seven predeclared comparators, including a non-deployable oracle, were not implemented, so
the remaining headroom is unmeasured.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

When a generative model is trained on text earlier models produced, the training loop
becomes self-consuming. Controlled studies show that some versions of the loop distort the
learned distribution, raise held-out error, and lose low-probability events
\cite{shumailov2024collapse,alemohammad2024mad,dohmatob2024tails}; others stay bounded, and
what separates them is the data workflow rather than synthetic data as such
\cite{gerstgrasser2024inevitable,kazdan2025collapse}. The failure is therefore a property
of the system's update rule, and the question is which interventions keep it bounded.

Retaining human-origin data is the most reliable known intervention
\cite{gerstgrasser2024inevitable,seddik2024synthetic}. But it is a finite resource:
expensive to license, curate and withhold from evaluation, and repeated optimizer exposure
to a small archive is not free. Give a chain one fixed lifetime stock of human-origin
\emph{optimizer-consumed} tokens and two decisions appear that the unconstrained setting
never forces --- \textbf{when} within the chain to spend, and \textbf{which} degraded
regions of the distribution to spend on. Prior work motivates each separately, through
schedules and learned mixtures \cite{xie2023doremi,liu2025regmix,zhao2026regmixd} and
through targeted or importance-weighted selection
\cite{xie2023dsir,ash2020badge,liu2021jtt}, but does not compare them, or their
combination, with both the lifetime human quantity and the total optimization exposure held
fixed. Appendix~\ref{app:related} develops the positioning.

\textbf{Contributions.} We formalise allocation of a fixed human-token stock as a joint
decision over recursive time and monitored modes under exact matching of both budgets
(Section~\ref{sec:problem}); decompose it into treatment families isolating each axis; and
execute the comparison on \PilotChains{} chains, reporting a preregistered null with an
interval inside the equivalence region alongside three admissible secondary contrasts
(Section~\ref{sec:results}). We also report what the design cannot support, rather than
leaving the gaps to inference (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{Problem and policies}
\label{sec:problem}

Over generations $g=0,\dots,G-1$ each model trains on the previous generation's decoded
corpus plus human examples bought by a policy, restarting optimization from the same
pretrained base so that what varies is the training distribution rather than accumulated
weights. With $b_{g,m}$ the human tokens from monitored mode $m$ consumed at generation
$g$, every policy satisfies $\sum_{g}\sum_{m} b_{g,m}=B$, counted per optimizer
presentation.

\textbf{Both budgets are matched.} Rescued human examples \emph{displace} synthetic records
rather than being appended, so each generation's training volume is fixed regardless of what
a policy buys. This is load-bearing: under additive assembly the no-rescue control trains on
strictly less data than every spending arm, and ``spending helps'' cannot then be separated
from ``more data helps''.

\textbf{Policies see only lagged state.} Allocation may use the generation index, remaining
budget, eligible candidates, the previous generation's monitoring statistics, and its own
history. Final-test data never influences prompting, selection, thresholds or stopping.
\emph{Fresh-random} follows a frozen schedule without mode targeting. \emph{Schedule-only}
varies timing but distributes each generation's spend neutrally across modes.
\emph{Selection-only} fixes timing but ranks candidates by their mode's lagged
under-coverage. \emph{Joint} adapts both. A \emph{no-rescue} control spends nothing.
Appendix~\ref{sec:method} gives the rules.

\textbf{Outcome.} With $L_g^{\pi}$ the held-out human NLL at generation $g$, regret is taken
against the chain's own generation-0 value, $r_g^{\pi}=L_g^{\pi}-L_0^{\pi}$, and the
chain-level outcome is the trapezoid area
$A^{\pi}=\sum_{g=1}^{G-1}\tfrac12(r_{g-1}^{\pi}+r_g^{\pi})$. The reference is within-chain,
so a paired contrast inherits no variance from a shared baseline. A frozen tail-retention
measure on a disjoint partition is confirmatory. One complete seeded chain is one
experimental unit; generations within a chain are repeated observations.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

GPT-2 on WikiText-103, horizon \PilotHorizon{}, the frozen seed set
$\{101,202,303,404,505\}$, and a \PilotCeiling{}-token lifetime budget per spending arm give
\PilotChains{} chains. Appendix~\ref{app:config} gives the executed configuration.

\textbf{Both fairness axes hold.} Realised human spend across spending arms spans
\PilotMatchedLow{}--\PilotMatchedHigh{} tokens, a \PilotHumanSpreadPct\% spread against
\PilotSpreadPermittedPct\% permitted; realised total optimizer tokens are \emph{identical}
across all five arms (\PilotTotalSpreadExactPct\%). All \PilotChains{} chains completed and
none certifies invalid, so the comparison is admissible.

\textbf{The primary contrast is a null.} The strongest eligible non-joint baseline is
\PilotPrimaryBaseline{}. Joint minus that baseline is \PilotPrimaryMean{}, $95\%$ Student
$t$ interval on four degrees of freedom $[\PilotPrimaryLow, \PilotPrimaryHigh]$, or
\PilotPrimaryPct\% relative, against a frozen equivalence region of
$\pm\PilotThresholdUnits{}$. The interval lies entirely inside that region, reaching at most
\PilotPrimaryReach{} from zero. This is the stronger form of a null: it excludes every
difference the preregistration calls meaningful, on a design powered to that threshold at
roughly three chains per arm and run with \PilotSeeds{}. Joint allocation is \emph{not}
shown to be worse --- the interval covers zero.

\textbf{Where the bound actually comes from.} Because all arms are matched, the secondary
contrasts stand on the same footing. Spending at all is worth \PilotRandNonePct\%,
CI $[\PilotRandNoneLow, \PilotRandNoneHigh]$, against a control trained on identical data
volume --- so the gain is attributable to the data's \emph{origin}, not its quantity.
Targeting degraded modes is worth a further \PilotSelRandPct\%,
CI $[\PilotSelRandLow, \PilotSelRandHigh]$. Scheduling gives \PilotSchedRandPct\%,
CI $[\PilotSchedRandLow, \PilotSchedRandHigh]$, an interval containing zero and inside the
equivalence region. The primary null follows: targeting accounts for most of the achievable
improvement, scheduling for none detectable, and combining them lands where targeting alone
lands.

\textbf{The confirmatory outcome agrees, with one exception we report.} On the primary
contrast tail retention agrees more tightly still (\PilotTailPrimaryMean{},
CI $[\PilotTailPrimaryLow,\allowbreak\ \PilotTailPrimaryHigh]$). On the \emph{timing}
contrast it disagrees: \PilotTailSchedRandMean{},
CI $[\PilotTailSchedRandLow,\allowbreak\ \PilotTailSchedRandHigh]$, excluding zero at
\PilotTailSchedRandPct\% of the fresh-random level. We do not select the more favourable
metric; the primary conclusion follows the primary outcome, and timing shows no detectable
effect on chain-level regret alongside a small, practically minor effect on tail retention.
Between-chain coefficients of variation run \PilotCVLow\%--\PilotCVHigh\% against the
\PilotThresholdPct\% threshold.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

For this self-consuming loop at this operating point, the intervention that bounds the
failure is \emph{diversity-aware targeting} of a finite human budget, not the schedule on
which it is spent. Adding temporal adaptation to distributional targeting buys nothing
detectable on the primary outcome.

Four limits bound that. \textbf{One operating point:} GPT-2, WikiText-103, horizon
\PilotHorizon{}, one budget, one assembly rule. \textbf{Missing comparators:} three of seven
predeclared comparators were not implemented --- accumulation or fixed-fraction mixing,
detector-based selection, and oracle mode information as a non-deployable upper bound. The
absent oracle is the consequential one: an equivalence between two policies says nothing
about how much of the achievable gain either captures, and a reader should not infer that
targeting sits near a ceiling. \textbf{An unarchived trace:} the retained artifacts record
how much each policy spent but not when or on which modes, so whether the joint policy
reached its total by a different route than selection-only cannot be determined --- an
equivalence between policies that allocated differently means something other than one
between policies that converged. \textbf{An unreviewed threshold:} the equivalence bound has
been checked against measured anchors and never externally reviewed; under the alternative
denominator it is $\pm\PilotThresholdUnitsAlt{}$ and the verdict is unchanged.

What would move this forward is not more chains --- variance is already below what the
threshold requires --- but the comparators that were not built, an oracle upper bound above
all, and operating points beyond the single one measured here.
