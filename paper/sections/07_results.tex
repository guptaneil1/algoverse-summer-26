\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{What was executed}

The corrected grid completed: five policies crossed with the \PilotSeeds{} preregistered
seeds, \PilotChains{} independently seeded chains, each run to the frozen horizon of
\PilotHorizon{} generations, on two accelerators. Every chain completed and none failed.
Wall time was \PilotWallHoursClean{} hours across the launches in which every chain
finished, and \PilotWallHoursIncludingFailed{} hours counting two earlier attempts that
infrastructure defects ended; both figures are read from the shard summaries rather than
from elapsed clock time.

All \PilotChains{} chains classify \texttt{valid\_with\_limitation} and none classifies
\texttt{invalid}. The recorded limitations are that the realised token ledger was compared
against the frozen budget rather than recomputed from batch records, and that
near-duplicate coverage was not rescanned at validation time. Both are standing properties
of the apparatus rather than of this run, and neither is blocking.

\subsection{Both fairness axes hold}

The fairness constraint has two axes: matched lifetime human-origin tokens \emph{and}
matched total optimizer tokens. A previous execution of this design failed both, and no
comparison from it was reportable. Under the corpus-assembly rule adopted for this run, in
which rescued human examples displace synthetic records rather than being appended to
them, both axes hold by construction.

Realised lifetime human-origin spend across the spending arms ranges from
\PilotMatchedLow{} to \PilotMatchedHigh{} optimizer tokens against a \PilotCeiling{}
ceiling, a spread of \PilotHumanSpreadPct\% against \PilotSpreadPermittedPct\% permitted.
Realised total optimizer tokens are identical across all five arms, a spread of
\PilotTotalSpreadExactPct\%. The control arm consumed exactly zero human tokens by
construction. The comparison is therefore admissible, and what follows is a result rather
than a precondition failure.

That the totals are identical rather than merely close is a property of the assembly rule,
not a coincidence: displacing one synthetic record per rescued human example holds each
generation's training volume fixed regardless of what a policy buys. It also removes a
confound the additive rule carried, in which the control trained on strictly less data than
every spending arm, so that ``spending helps'' could not be separated from ``more data
helps''.

\subsection{The primary comparison: a null}

The preregistered primary contrast is the paired chain-level difference in held-out human
NLL-regret AUC between the joint policy and the strongest eligible non-joint baseline, with
lower values favouring joint. The strongest eligible non-joint baseline in this run is
\PilotPrimaryBaseline{}, identified by lowest mean regret among the four non-joint arms;
the selection rule is preregistered, the arm it selects is read from the outcome.

At matched budgets across \PilotChains{} chains, we did not detect a difference between
joint allocation and the strongest non-joint baseline: paired mean difference
\PilotPrimaryMean{}, $95\%$ Student $t$ interval on four degrees of freedom
$[\PilotPrimaryLow, \PilotPrimaryHigh]$, or \PilotPrimaryPct\% relative. Every interval
reported in this section is of that form, paired by seed over complete chains. The frozen practical equivalence region is
$\pm\PilotThresholdUnits{}$ in outcome units, and the interval lies entirely inside it,
reaching at most \PilotPrimaryReach{} from zero.

\textbf{We report this as a null result, not as a trend.} It is moreover the stronger form
of a null: the interval does not merely include zero, it excludes every difference the
preregistration calls practically meaningful. The design is powered to detect effects at
the preregistered threshold with roughly three chains per arm, and it ran with
\PilotSeeds{}. The claim that joint allocation improves on the strongest non-joint baseline
is \textbf{not supported}. Joint allocation is not shown to be worse either; the finding is
practical equivalence to selection-only.

The verdict does not depend on how the equivalence region's denominator is read. Taken
against the strongest baseline rather than against fresh random it is
$\pm\PilotThresholdUnitsAlt{}$, and the interval lies inside that too. Both figures are
emitted by the analysis pipeline so the choice can be checked rather than trusted.

\subsection{What does move the outcome}

Table~\ref{tab:pilot-results} gives the per-arm figures the contrasts below are computed
from. Because all five arms are matched on both axes, the secondary contrasts are
admissible on the same footing as the primary one. Three of them are informative, and
together they locate where the primary null comes from.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Spending human tokens at all helps.} Fresh random against the no-rescue
      control improves the outcome by \PilotRandNonePct\%, $95\%$ CI
      $[\PilotRandNoneLow, \PilotRandNoneHigh]$, clearing the practical threshold. Under
      the displacing assembly rule this arm trains on exactly as much data as the control,
      so the improvement is attributable to the human origin of part of that data rather
      than to its quantity.

\item \textbf{Which examples are bought matters, and matters more.} Selection-only against
      fresh random improves the outcome by \PilotSelRandPct\%, $95\%$ CI
      $[\PilotSelRandLow, \PilotSelRandHigh]$, with identical total training volume
      (\PilotSelRandTotalPct\% difference). Targeting under-covered modes is worth roughly
      twice again what unselective spending is worth.

\item \textbf{When the budget is spent does not.} Schedule-only against fresh random gives
      \PilotSchedRandPct\%, $95\%$ CI $[\PilotSchedRandLow, \PilotSchedRandHigh]$, an
      interval containing zero and lying inside the practical equivalence region, at
      matched human spend (\PilotSchedRandHumanPct\%) and matched totals
      (\PilotSchedRandTotalPct\%). This replicates the one clean contrast available from
      the earlier execution, now with both axes holding rather than one.
\end{itemize}

The primary null follows from these. Selection accounts for most of the achievable
improvement, scheduling accounts for none that we can detect on the primary outcome, and
the joint policy that combines them lands where selection alone lands. At this operating
point the allocation question decomposes: \emph{which} under-covered modes a fixed human
budget is spent on changes the outcome, and \emph{when} within the chain it is spent does
not.

\subsection{The confirmatory outcome}

The preregistration names a frozen tail-retention metric as confirmatory. On the primary
contrast it agrees, and more tightly: joint minus the strongest baseline is
\PilotTailPrimaryMean{}, $95\%$ CI $[\PilotTailPrimaryLow, \PilotTailPrimaryHigh]$.

On one secondary contrast it disagrees. Schedule-only against fresh random is
\PilotTailSchedRandMean{}, $95\%$ CI $[\PilotTailSchedRandLow, \PilotTailSchedRandHigh]$
--- an interval excluding zero, where the primary outcome shows none. The effect is
\PilotTailSchedRandPct\% of the fresh-random level, below the relative bar the
preregistration fixes for the primary outcome, though that bar was set for that outcome and
its transfer to this one is not something the preregistration settles. We do not select the
more favourable metric: the primary conclusion follows the primary outcome, and the honest
summary is that timing shows no detectable effect on chain-level regret and a small,
statistically distinguishable, practically minor effect on tail retention. It is reported
because it was measured.

\subsection{Trajectories}

Figure~\ref{fig:pilot-nll} plots held-out human NLL against generation for each policy.
Two features carry the section's findings in visual form: every arm that spends its budget
ends below the no-rescue control, and the selection-only and joint trajectories lie on top
of one another for the whole horizon, which is the primary null rather than a plotting
artefact.

\subsection{Between-chain variance}

Coefficients of variation on the primary outcome range from \PilotCVLow\% to \PilotCVHigh\%
across the five arms, against a preregistered practical effect threshold of
\PilotThresholdPct\%. The paired difference between the joint policy and the strongest
baseline has a standard deviation of \PilotPairedSD{} in outcome units, where the practical
threshold corresponds to \PilotThresholdUnits{} in those units.

This reproduces, on an independent grid run under a changed corpus-assembly rule, the
variance estimate obtained from the earlier execution. Chain count is not the binding
constraint on a powered study at this threshold: the frozen seed set exceeds what the
preregistered effect size requires. Unlike the earlier estimate, the joint policy's
variance here was measured on chains that spent their budget as designed, so the
qualification that previously attached to that arm no longer applies.

\subsection{Scope of these results}

Four limits bound every comparison above. They hold at one budget, one horizon, one corpus
and one model, and nothing here speaks to larger models, longer chains, or different budget
ratios. Three of the seven comparators the claim contract names were not implemented ---
accumulation or fixed-fraction mixing, detector-based or importance-resampling selection,
and oracle mode information as a non-deployable upper bound --- so ``strongest eligible
baseline'' means strongest among those that exist. The absent oracle in particular means
the headroom remaining above any of these policies is unknown, and an equivalence between
two policies says nothing about how far either is from what is achievable. Finally, the
practical equivalence threshold on which the primary null rests is a project decision that
has been checked against measured anchors but never externally reviewed.

\subsection{Positive control}

The published positive control reproduces qualitatively. Both arms complete every planned
generation; the expected arm ordering and degradation direction are recovered; a clean
rerun on independent hardware reaches the same conclusion, with every reported quantity
agreeing to well under one per cent across the two executions; all deviations are
enumerated; and the reported table is regenerated from saved metrics by the same command
that produced them, with every recorded artifact hash verified.

Agreement with the published endpoint is assessed against a five per cent relative
difference adopted as an internal engineering tolerance. It is not proof of exact
replication, and no downstream claim rests on it.

This establishes that the pipeline reproduces a known result on real assets. Together with
the executed grid it establishes that the apparatus runs the full recursive allocation loop
end to end on real models under matched budgets and produces certifiable artifacts.
