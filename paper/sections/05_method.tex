\section{Method}

\paragraph{Recursive chain and policy interface.}
Let $g\in\{1,\ldots,G\}$ index recursive generations and let $m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}$ index modes of a monitoring partition fixed before primary outcomes are viewed. At generation $g$, the preceding model generates a synthetic pool under a frozen prompt and decoding configuration. A policy receives only the policy-visible state: the generation index, remaining human-token budget, prior allocations, eligible rescue-candidate metadata, and frozen monitoring statistics derived from validation data. The policy cannot inspect the final-test partition, future generations, confirmation outcomes, or fields that directly encode the answer. It returns selected stable example identifiers and presentation counts. The runner, rather than the policy, resolves those identifiers into tokenized batches and records the realized allocation.

\paragraph{Accounting and feasibility.}
For an eligible human example $i$, let $\ell_i$ be its non-padding token count and $r_{g,i}$ the number of optimizer presentations at generation $g$. Human use is counted as $\sum_i r_{g,i}\ell_i$; repeated epochs therefore spend the budget repeatedly. If $b_{g,m}$ is the realized count for monitored mode $m$, every budget-matched nonzero policy must satisfy
\[
  \sum_{g=1}^{G}\sum_{m=1}^{M} b_{g,m}=B.
\]
The total number of human- and synthetic-origin tokens consumed by the optimizer is also matched at $T$. Padding is excluded under the frozen attention-mask rule. Token counts are accumulated from batches actually processed, checkpointed with the run, and checked again after resume. A proposal that exceeds the remaining budget is rejected rather than silently truncated. If exact packing cannot fill the final allowance from eligible examples, the predeclared deterministic packing or replacement rule must be used; \textbf{PENDING\_POLICY\_FREEZE:} Aarav's final rule and budget values must replace this marker before preregistration.

\paragraph{Four policy families.}
The \emph{fresh-random} policy follows a fixed generation schedule and samples eligible human examples using a seeded, mode-neutral rule. It measures whether unstructured access to the same human-token exposure is sufficient. The \emph{schedule-only} family may adapt the generation total $b_g=\sum_m b_{g,m}$ using the visible chain state, but within a generation it must follow the same frozen neutral mode distribution. The \emph{selection-only} family follows a fixed vector $(b_1,\ldots,b_G)$ while adapting which monitored modes and examples receive the allocation. The \emph{joint} family may adapt both $b_g$ and the conditional allocation across modes. These definitions separate the value of timing, targeting, and their coupling. A no-rescue chain is a diagnostic reference, not a budget-matched competitor. Accumulation, fixed-fraction mixing, detector-based resampling, and an oracle-mode policy are included where the frozen protocol makes them scientifically and computationally feasible.

\paragraph{Monitoring, selection, and bias.}
The monitoring map $\mu(x)$ is created from rescue-eligible human data without final-test access. Before allocating at generation $g$, the policy may observe mode counts and one frozen under-coverage statistic computed from validation or lagged generation artifacts. The same statistic, normalization, clipping, and minimum-support rule must apply to every adaptive policy. Candidate tie-breaking is deterministic after conditioning on the chain seed: first by score, then by stable example identifier. Mode ties use the frozen mode identifier. A candidate cannot move between partitions, and no generated or final-test record can be relabeled as human rescue. The primary monitor is fixed; a separate omission condition hides one preregistered globally important mode from the policy while leaving evaluation unchanged. This intervention tests failure under reference misspecification rather than improving the primary policy.

\paragraph{State transitions and reproducibility.}
Each policy call produces a versioned allocation record containing the input-state hash, policy/config hash, selected IDs, scores visible at decision time, planned token count, realized token count, remaining budget, and seed. The runner writes the allocation before training, updates the immutable run manifest after artifacts are hashed, and checkpoints model, optimizer, scheduler, random-number-generator, accounting, and policy history. Resume must reproduce the frozen equivalence criterion; otherwise the chain is invalid or analyzed under an explicitly approved tolerance. Policies cannot mutate their configs after launch, and analysis consumes only schema-valid completed chain results.

\paragraph{Primary comparison.}
One independently seeded complete chain is one experimental unit. Policies are paired on starting checkpoint, data manifests, generation prompts, seed, $G$, $B$, $T$, model, tokenizer, and evaluation configuration. The primary contrast compares the joint policy with the strongest eligible non-joint policy selected by a screening rule frozen before confirmation outcomes. Generation-wise observations are treated as repeated measurements within a chain. \textbf{RESULT\_PENDING:} the exact policy equations, primary horizon, lifetime budget, mode construction, meaningful-effect threshold, and ordered seed list remain visibly pending until their owners freeze them; none may be chosen from novel-treatment outcomes.
