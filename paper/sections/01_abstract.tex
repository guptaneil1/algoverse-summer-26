\begin{abstract}
Recursive training on model-generated data degrades a language model, and retaining
human-origin data in the mixture substantially reduces that degradation. This leaves a
question open: under a \emph{fixed lifetime budget} of human-origin optimizer tokens, when
during recursive training should those tokens be spent, and which under-covered modes of a
human reference distribution should they target? We predefine four budget-matched treatment
families --- random rescue, schedule-only, selection-only, and a joint time-and-mode policy
--- with complete recursive chains as experimental units, and freeze the allocation rule,
seed set, outcomes, and interpretation language before execution. The pipeline is validated
against a published positive control, reproducing its arm ordering and degradation
direction across two independent executions.

We then execute the pilot: \PilotChains{} chains over \PilotSeeds{} preregistered seeds and
\PilotHorizon{} generations. The preregistered fairness constraint requires equal lifetime
human-origin tokens \emph{and} equal total optimizer tokens; this run satisfies neither,
with one policy under-spending by \PilotJointShortfallPct\% and realised totals spanning
\PilotTotalSpreadPct\% across arms. We report the primary contrast as invalid rather than
repairing or reweighting it, and we decline the secondary comparison that would have read
more favourably. One comparison survives both constraints and is a null: at this budget and
horizon, \emph{when} the budget is spent does not detectably change the outcome.
Between-chain variance proves small enough that the frozen seed set is already powered at
the preregistered threshold, inverting the assumption that chain count would bind. We make
no claim about allocation policy. The contribution is the design, an apparatus validated on
real models, and a record of checks that caught what they were built to catch --- including
the one that invalidated our own primary result before it was read.
\end{abstract}
